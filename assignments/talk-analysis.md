# Talk Analysis

**Due:** before class on Tuesday, September 22. Individual. **Pass/redo.**

## Why this exists

In three weeks you will start giving feedback on classmates' talks, and in a few more you will write a post-mortem on your own. Both are easier with a shared vocabulary. This assignment puts Duarte's vocabulary in your hands on a talk where nobody's feelings are involved.

## Pick a talk

- A recorded talk of **20 minutes or less**.
- Given to a **real audience** in a room: a conference talk, a TED or TEDx talk, a lecture, a startup pitch, a keynote. Not a polished explainer video, screencast, or tutorial. The point is to watch someone try to move a room.
- Any subject. Technical is fine; so is anything else.
- Include a link to the video at the top of your write-up.

Good sources: TED, a conference you care about (Strange Loop, PyCon, JavaOne, GDC), the lectures Duarte discusses in the book.

## Write one to two pages covering

1. **The ask.** What did the speaker want the audience to believe or do afterward? Say it in one sentence. If you cannot, that is a finding.
2. **The hero.** Who was the talk for, and did the speaker treat the audience as the hero or themselves? Point to a moment.
3. **The shape.** Where did the talk move between *what is* and *what could be*? Sketch the sparkline if that helps. Where was the S.T.A.R. moment, if there was one?
4. **What landed.** One or two specific moments that worked, and why in Duarte's terms.
5. **What did not.** One or two specific moments that fell flat, and why.
6. **One change.** If you could give the speaker one note before they gave the talk again, what would it be?

Use timestamps. "At 4:10 she switches from the problem to a story about a patient" is evidence; "the middle was good" is not.

## Pass or redo

A pass is specific, uses the vocabulary correctly, and shows you watched the whole talk. A redo goes back to you with a note and a few days to resubmit. Common reasons for a redo: no clear ask, no timestamps, or observations that could apply to any talk.
